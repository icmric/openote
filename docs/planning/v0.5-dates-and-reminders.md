# Openote v0.5 — dates, reminders and the planner

> **Document status:** Draft v0.1 · Planning · 2026-08-05
> **Owner:** Eric · **Related:** [v0.4 backlog](v0.4-and-beyond.md) · [v0.3 student plan](v0.3-student-plan.md) · [ADR-0006 §8](../adr/ADR-0006-sync-transport-and-text-model.md)
> **Sizes:** **S** ≈ hours · **M** ≈ 1–3 days · **L** ≈ ~a week · **XL** ≈ multi-week.

## 0. Why this document exists now

The v0.3 plan cut this explicitly:

> *"task-manager creep (due-date/agenda views were considered and **cut** — a
> date on a tag is a slippery slope into a planner; revisit only on real user
> demand)."*

That was the right call at the time and it carried a revisit trigger. **The
trigger has fired**: the exam countdown shipped, was used, and the report back
was that managing it is awkward, that a calendar would help, and that reminders
and quick "come back to this" tasks are wanted. So the slope is being walked
deliberately, with the brakes written down in §6.

## 1. The constraint that shapes everything: reminders cannot rely on the OS

Researched before designing, because it inverts the obvious architecture.

`flutter_local_notifications` is BSD-3-Clause (compatible with both licence
tiers) and supports all three desktops — but **not equally**:

| Platform | Scheduling | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| macOS | ✅ `zonedSchedule` works | UserNotifications framework |
| Windows | ⚠️ partial | **no repeating notifications**; needs **MSIX package identity** to retrieve or cancel previously shown ones |
| **Linux** | ❌ **none** | *"Scheduled/pending notifications is currently not supported due to the lack of a scheduler API"* |

Openote ships a portable `.zip` on Windows, an AppImage on Linux and a `.dmg`
on macOS — **no MSIX identity anywhere**. And Linux is not a nice-to-have here:
*"no native Linux client"* is one of the founding complaints in the product
vision, and design principle 5 is *"native feel on every platform —
cross-platform, not lowest-common-denominator"*.

**So the decision is:**

> **Openote owns the schedule. The OS notification is a display channel, never
> the scheduler.**

Reminders live in Openote's own store, are evaluated by Openote's own timer, and
fire in-app. An OS toast may *additionally* be raised when the app is running,
on whichever platforms support it, because that costs nothing and is strictly
better where available. Nothing depends on it.

### What that means honestly

A desktop app that is not running cannot interrupt you. Pretending otherwise
would be the worst outcome — a student who trusts a reminder that never
arrives is worse off than one who never had it. So:

- A reminder due while Openote is open **pops up**.
- A reminder that came due while it was closed appears in a **catch-up list on
  next open** — *"3 reminders while you were away"* — as a first-class state,
  not an afterthought. This is what makes the feature trustworthy rather than
  unreliable.
- The wording throughout is a **study nudge, not an alarm clock**. It never
  claims to be the latter.

Package choice (`flutter_local_notifications` vs the MIT `local_notifier`) is
deliberately deferred to implementation: the architecture does not depend on
it, and neither can schedule on Linux.

## 2. The insight: the tasks already exist

Openote already has tasks. They are **tagged lines**:

- `TagKind.todo` with a real `checked` state, click-to-complete in the gutter.
- Tags **rebase** when you edit around them (`NoteTag.rebase`), so a task stays
  attached to the sentence it is about.
- Tags **sync**, riding inside block content — no new op kind needed.
- There is already a **find-tags rollup** listing every tagged line in the
  notebook.

What they lack is a **date** and an **agenda**. So the design is not "add a task
system"; it is "give the tasks you already write a due date, and a view that
sorts by it". That is the same move flashcards made — a *view* of your notes
rather than a second system to maintain — and it is why this can be small.

**Recommended:** an optional `due` on the tag, plus an optional personal
reminder time.

### Where each piece of data lives, and why

| Data | Home | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Due date** on a task | the tag, in block content (**synced**) | A deadline is a property of the task itself. In a shared notebook the group's deadline is the same deadline. Additive to the tag JSON, so older readers ignore it and the frozen v1 format is untouched. |
| **Reminder time** ("nudge me at 4pm") | workspace settings (**not synced**) | When *you* want to be interrupted is personal — the identical argument already used for card schedules and exam dates. Pushing it through the op log would make one person's alarm everyone's. |
| **Exam date** on a section | workspace settings today | Stays personal, but stops being a private corner of the study panel — it becomes one *kind* of dated item in the agenda (§3). |

## 3. The surface: a Planner, and exam dates stop hiding in it

Today an exam date is set from two different context menus and is visible only
inside the study panel, on the section you happen to be looking at. There is no
way to see *"what dates do I have at all"*. That is the "not easy to use"
complaint, and it is a surfacing problem rather than a data one.

**The Planner** — one panel, reachable from the navigator's Home pane (which
already exists and already holds favourites and recents):

```
PLANNER
─────────────────────────────────────
TODAY                        Thu 5 Aug
  🎯 Discrete Maths exam · 14 days
     40 to learn · 3 a day covers it
  ☐ Finish tutorial 4        Week 3 ▸
  🔔 Re-read the proof of 2.7      4pm

THIS WEEK
  ☐ Draft the report         Project ▸
  🎯 Networks exam · 9 days

LATER  ·  3 more
─────────────────────────────────────
  + Add a date…
```

Every row clicks through to the note it came from. Every row can be dated,
re-dated or cleared from here — one place, which is the actual fix for the
exam-date complaint.

## 4. Calendar: subscribe to the real one, don't rebuild it

Two very different readings of "integrate a calendar", and the valuable one is
not the obvious one.

**A month grid inside Openote** is presentation. Worth having, cheap once the
data model exists, and deliberately *second* — a grid is a lot of layout before
any of the model is exercised, and an agenda is what a student actually reads
daily.

**Your university's real timetable** is the one that would change the product.
Lecture times, assessment due dates and exam windows already exist in a system
the student did not choose. The right integration is **ICS**:

- Nearly every university LMS and timetabling system publishes a subscribable
  `.ics` URL, and so do Google Calendar, Outlook and Apple Calendar.
- It is a **plain GET of a text file** — no OAuth, no account, no SDK. That
  matters: [ADR-0006 §8](../adr/ADR-0006-sync-transport-and-text-model.md)
  rejected cloud *APIs* in favour of folders precisely to avoid becoming an
  OAuth client, and an ICS URL keeps that promise. Openote never gets access to
  anything except the calendar you pasted.
- Read-only, one direction. Openote shows your timetable beside your notes and
  never writes to your calendar.
- It also gives exam dates for free: subscribe once and the exam countdown
  stops being something you type in by hand.

**Recommended:** ICS subscription (URL or local file), read-only, refreshed on
open. Not Google Calendar OAuth — that is a different product with an account
requirement this project exists to avoid.

## 5. Suggested staging

Each stage is independently shippable and each earns the next.

| # | Stage | Size | Why here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Planner panel (agenda)** + move exam dates into it | **M** | Fixes the actual complaint. Pure surfacing over data that already exists — no new storage, no new dependency. |
| 2 | **Due dates on to-do tags** | **M** | Turns the tags you already write into the agenda's content. Additive to the tag JSON. |
| 3 | **Reminders** (in-app + catch-up on open) | **M** | Openote's own scheduler and store, per §1. OS toast added opportunistically. |
| 4 | **ICS subscription** | **M–L** | The timetable arrives on its own. Needs a small ICS parser (RFC 5545 subset: `VEVENT`, `DTSTART`, `SUMMARY`, `RRULE` for weekly lectures) — a vendored parser or a licence-audited package. |
| 5 | **Month grid** | **S–M** | Cheap once 1–4 exist; pure presentation over the same model. |

Stage 1 alone answers *"the exam countdown is not that easy to use"*. Stages
1–3 answer everything reported.

## 6. The brakes — what this must not become

Recorded because the v0.3 plan's instinct was right even though its conclusion
has been overtaken. Openote is a notebook, not a planner, and the line is:

- **No task that isn't in your notes.** Every dated item is a view of something
  you wrote, or an event from a calendar you subscribed to. Free-standing
  tasks belonging to nothing are how this becomes a to-do app with a notes
  feature. *(This is the one point most worth arguing about — see §7.)*
- **No projects, no priorities, no assignees, no sub-tasks, no recurrence
  editor.** If it needs a schema of its own, it is out of scope.
- **No writing to anyone's calendar.** Read-only, one direction, always.
- **No account, ever** — the ICS URL is a string the user pastes, not a login.
- **The agenda is a lens, not a store.** Deleting the note deletes the task,
  because they were never two things.

## 7. Open decisions

1. **Reminder reach** — recommended: in-app + catch-up, identical on all three
   platforms. Alternative: add OS scheduling where it works (macOS fully,
   Windows partly), accepting per-platform behaviour. A third option, MSIX
   packaging for Windows, changes release engineering and still leaves Linux
   out.
2. **Free-standing reminders** — §6 says every dated item hangs off a note.
   *"Call the tutor at 3"* is a real thing a student wants and belongs to no
   page. Allowing it is a genuine loosening of the rule; the cheap compromise
   is that such a reminder creates a line on a daily note, so it is still
   *somewhere* rather than nowhere.
3. **Whether stage 4 (ICS) should jump the queue.** It is the highest-value
   piece for a student and the most differentiating, but also the only one
   with an external dependency and a network call.
