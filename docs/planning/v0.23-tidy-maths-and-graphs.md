### 2. The interface

- **B7** The Insert menu has redundant entries (a text box is what a left
  click already makes) and "just feels kinda messy".
- **B8** The top bar moves you to the Maths tab the moment you enter an
  equation. Nothing should move you without your say-so. Rethink the bar
  itself — designs to be explored, tame and radical, before anything is built.
- **B9** The canvas right-click menu says "insert X here"; "here" is implied
  by the right click. Drop it, drop the text box, and bring it closer to the
  Insert menu without becoming a large drop-down.
- **B10** A consistency sweep, against the owner's stated principles:
  1. as simple and easy for the user as possible;
  2. as fast and responsive as possible — assume success and show a failure
     if it comes, or show progress where that is better;
  3. never move the user or change things without their command;
  4. consistent behaviour — *"A regular user is not going to think a
     standalone maths box is any different to one in a text box, so they must
     have exact feature and behaviour parrody."*
- **B11** Instructions carry as little text as they can: icons and mapping
  over sentences, and where words are needed, the fewest that work — the
  model being `symbol (\symbol)` rather than `symbol — type \symbol`.

### 3. Tests

Comprehensive tests over everything added or changed above, before moving on.

### 4. The maths that is missing

- **B12** nth roots: `\sqrt` stays, `\cbrt` for a cube root, `\rt` for a
  generic root with an index box, `\nrt` as the shorthand that fills the
  index in, and `\2rt` renders the 2 even though a bare radical means 2.
  Rendering is the requirement; calculating is a bonus.
- **B13** Multi-argument functions, with the shape filled in on insert:
  `\gcd` writes `gcd(□,□)` using the same empty boxes as everywhere else.
  Only for functions that *require* more than one argument — `sin` does not.
  Where a convention is unclear, follow a Casio fx-82AU PLUS II.
- **B14** *Not a priority.* A "computer" family — binary, hex, octal, decimal
  and conversion between them — only if it can be added without clutter.

### 5. Graphs

- **B15** 2D graphing, live as you type (no debounce needed at this cost).
- **B16** Write `y = 3x+10` as maths, select it, "Graph this" — a *new* graph
  element appears on the page. It moves independently, and stays tied to that
  equation: change it to `y = 2x+6` and the graph follows.
- **B17** The link is shown by selection and only by selection: click the
  graph and it takes a subtle themed border while its equation takes a
  background tint of the same colour; click the equation and both light up
  the same way. Never visible unless one of them is selected *and* a link
  exists.
- **B18** 3D is explicitly optional — *"if its going to require its own
  seperate system compared to 2d stuff and will add a lot of complexity,
  lets not bother for now."*
- Benchmarks to keep in mind: Desmos and Wolfram Alpha, being where a student
  would otherwise go.

## Phase 0 findings — probed, not guessed

**B1 — the DEG/RAD button throws you out of the equation.** Root cause found
and proven. `AppState.setAngleMode` bumps `docRevision`
(`app/lib/state/app_state.dart:4257`), and `page_canvas.dart:944` keys every
block widget by `ValueKey('id#docRevision')`. Bumping it destroys and rebuilds
every block on the page — the equation editor is disposed, its focus node with
it, and the caret is gone. `docRevision` means "the stored content was
replaced wholesale"; an angle mode is not that. A press on a palette chip
survives only because `MathField.insertItem` re-requests focus by hand
(`math_field.dart:150`), which is a per-button remedy for a bar-wide rule:
**the toolbar must never take the caret**, and that has to be structural or
the next button added repeats the bug.

**B6 — "the calculating didnt seem to work when inline, at least for trig".**
Not reproducible as reported. Every inline route works in a real-AppState
harness: typed backslash-sin then `(30)= `, plain-letter `sin(30)= ` (the `(`
converts the word), the palette's own sin chip, `sin 30` with no brackets,
Alt+= into a fresh equation, tan and cos, and the debounced re-work after an
edit. The likely truth is that this IS B1: clicking DEG/RAD dropped them out
of the equation, so everything typed afterwards went nowhere. Radians mode is
the other candidate — `sin(30)` in radians is −0.988, which reads as broken.
Either way, a regression test for each of those routes now belongs in the
suite so the claim stays checkable.

Noticed while probing, and worth fixing:

- `cos(45)` answers `0.707106781187` — **12 significant figures**, where a
  Casio shows 10. This is what B4's "number of significant figures" is for,
  and 12 should not be the default.
- `app/lib/editor/text_block_view.dart` contains five literal NUL bytes
  (a cache-key separator, deliberate) which make **ripgrep treat the file as
  binary and skip it** — it is invisible to every code search in the repo.
  The unicode escape reads the same to Dart and keeps the file text.
- The Maths tab is entered automatically: `CommandBar._effectiveTab` returns
  `_mathsTab` whenever `_mathsOpen` (`command_bar.dart:97`). That is B8.


## The interface decision — **the object row**

Five designs were produced (two conventional, three radical) and judged by
three lenses: a year-10 student who has used OneNote and Docs, a university
student writing their hundredth equation on a 1280-wide laptop, and the
engineer who has to build it in this codebase.

| Design | Student | Power user | Engineer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Four Tabs, One Strip | 7 | 7 | 8 |
| Bar and Shelf | 6 | 8 | 7 |
| Add & Alongside | 5 | 5 | 5 |
| Cuff and Drawer | 4 | 6 | 6 |
| The Bench | 8 | 8 | 8 |

**Built: THE OBJECT ROW** — the first design's contextual strip, made
*permanent*, with the View tab retired to pay for it. One sentence carries it:

> Bands A and B belong to the student. Band C belongs to the page — and the
> page lends it to an equation while the student is writing one.

Chrome is **32 + 44 + 36 = 112 px in every state of the app**. Nothing grows,
nothing shrinks, nothing re-points, and the canvas box never moves. That is
what makes "don't move the user" a property of the layout rather than a
promise someone has to keep.

- **Band A** — three permanent tabs: Home, Insert, Draw. Plus a *badge* while
  an equation is open — a pill that says `ƒ Equation`, is never underlined,
  and **cannot be clicked**, so it cannot be misread as a fifth tab.
  `_effectiveTab`, `_leftMaths` and `_mathsTab` are deleted; `_tab` is written
  only by a tap on a tab.
- **Band B** — the command row, unchanged. If you were on Home when you
  started an equation, Home is what you are still looking at.
- **Band C** — the object row. At rest it carries the page's own controls
  (background, canvas/paper, snap, zoom); while an equation is being written
  it carries `MathBar` **verbatim**; when graphs land it carries their
  controls. Only its contents cross-fade, 90 ms, opacity only.
- **Insert** — one catalog (`insert_catalog.dart`) rendered twice: as the
  ribbon and as the right-click menu, so "the menu matches Insert" is true by
  construction. Ten items in three groups (Write · Bring in · Link up). Text
  box, Flashcard and Template are deleted from Insert — a left click already
  makes a text box, Home's own card button reads the line you are on, and
  applying a page template is not inserting a block.
- **The canvas menu** — Paste, then those same ten as a three-column grid,
  then `Page background ▸`. Roughly 420 × 200, which is *shorter* than the
  eleven-row stack it replaces. The word "here" is gone from every label.
- **Parity, mechanically** — `EquationFace`'s constructor takes an
  `ActiveMathEditor` and primitives only: no `AppState`, no `Block`, no
  `BlockType`, no placement. A block equation and one in a sentence cannot be
  told apart, because nothing downstream is given anything to tell them apart
  with.
- **Retiring View** — once the page controls live on Band C, the View tab
  holds four preferences, and the app already says settings live in Settings.
  Theme, spell check, AI access and keyboard shortcuts move there.

Costs taken knowingly: 36 px more chrome (112 against 76); the page controls
are not reachable from the row while an equation is open (`Ctrl` `+` `-` `0`
still are); and anyone who had learned Insert ▸ Flashcard or Insert ▸ Template
loses their entrance, so the release note has to lead with where they went.

**The two roads not taken.** *The Bench* — a permanent 320 px dock on the
right holding the whole palette like a calculator on the desk — scored highest
with the student and tied highest overall, and its best idea (press a symbol
with a text caret and an inline equation starts, seeded with that symbol) is
worth building anyway. It lost on arithmetic: 320 of 1280 px is a quarter of
the page, permanently, for a student taking English notes. *Bar and Shelf*
deleted the tabs entirely and kept chrome at today's 76 px, but deleting the
four tabs deletes the four words that tell a beginner what the app can do, and
formatting becomes invisible at rest.

### Build order (each step ships a working app)

0. Lift the ten insert actions into `insert_catalog.dart`; both surfaces call
   the same function. Nothing visibly changes.
1. Insert regrouped to ten in three groups; the deletions land; the canvas
   menu is rebuilt. **Complaints 1 and 3 done.**
2. `object_row.dart` + `object_face.dart`, page face only, permanent.
3. The equation face; delete the contextual tab and the hijack; the badge.
   **Complaint 2 done.**
4. Delete the View tab; Settings absorbs the four preferences.
5. Keyboard: an object-row region, `Shift+F10`, `Ctrl+Space`, focus into and
   out of the symbol panels.
6. *(with graphs)* one enum arm and a graph face.
