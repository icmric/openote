Three backlog items were opened as one cluster:

- **#39** — demote the container to `cache.onote` (ADR-0006 §3)
- **#54** — Phase 2: log segments, gzip, snapshots, own-history compaction
- **#55** — Phase 3+5: demote container to `cache.onote`; video engine on demand

They were costed in August against a world that **Phase 1 (#53, commit
9c1c301) has since replaced**. Four investigation passes and two spikes
re-measured every number on the owner's real notebooks. This document is the
result: one task is closed outright, one is split in three, and the part that
survives is re-argued as a *correctness* fix that happens to save bytes.

**The headline, in one line:** the op log is now a proven rebuild authority
(329 of 329 real pages replay byte-identically in 120 ms), the size prize has
shrunk from ~93 MB to ~30 MB per shared notebook, and every way a spike managed
to destroy a notebook was in the *bookkeeping around* the migration, never in
the format change itself.

# Part 1 — What we are NOT doing

Cutting scope on evidence is the most valuable output of this investigation.
Five things in the cluster are cut. Each is recorded here so nobody
re-discovers them in six months.

## 1.1 #54 in its entirety — **close it**

The task is four mechanisms. All four were measured on the real logs, and one
of them was reproduced destroying real data.

| Mechanism | What it was for | Measured today | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Log segments** (rotate at 8 MB) | GitHub's 100 MB single-file reject; whole-file re-reads | Live log is **1,523,044 B** — 1.5% of the limit. Rotation would never fire. Segmenting the current log to 256 KiB leaves CPU unchanged (33–37 ms vs 31 ms) and **triples wall time** (115 ms vs 53 ms), because the paced fold's wall clock is set by total bytes, not file count. For git it is *worse*: `.git` 344 KiB segmented vs 337 KiB monolithic after `gc`, because separate files defeat cross-segment delta. | **Cut** |
| **gzip closed segments** | Shrink the synced set | 5.52× on log text, but **1.00× on ink blobs** (already a zlib level-9 stream inside the OIS1 container — confirmed positively by inflating 20 blobs: 716,478 → 1,274,966 B) and **1.05× on PNG**. Gzipping everything gzip can help saves **1.98 MiB of a 34.95 MiB notebook (5.7%)**. It cannot touch the active tail: a compressed active segment turns a 522-byte append into a **212–322 KB rewrite every 700 ms autosave pause** (400–600× write amplification). And a truncated gzip member loses up to a whole member where a truncated line loses one op — trading away the exact property JSON Lines was chosen for. | **Cut** |
| **Snapshots** | Get open-replay under ~150 ms | Rebuilding the entire 329-page notebook from its log is **54 ms of CPU** (33 read+decode+sort, 2 materialize, 20 encode 329 mirrors); end-to-end 97–120 ms, in a background isolate. There is nothing to optimise. A snapshot would also *add* bytes inside the synced tree and, per ADR-0006 §3's `snapshots/`, superseding one means deleting a synced file. | **Cut** |
| **Own-history compaction** | Reclaim superseded bytes | Superseded bytes are **0.00% of Honours-4's log**, **0.16% (0.104 MB) of Honours-2's 64.56 MB log**, and **143 KB** of My Notebook's 268 KB. It reaches 93% only on edit-driven history, and the only edit-driven log in existence is 274.6 KiB total. | **Cut** |

Compaction is also the only one that can lose notes, and a spike reproduced
that **on the owner's real 1.5 MiB log**, not on a synthetic one:

- **Byte-offset corruption.** `readDeviceFrom`'s only guard is
  `from <= len` — a guard against a *shorter* file, not a *different* one.
  After device A compacts (1,585,183 → 1,567,083 B), a peer resuming at its
  stored offset silently lost **7 blocks at a 25% read point, 10 blocks + 3
  nodes + 2 blob refs at 50%, 24 blocks + 1 node + 1 blob ref at 75%**. No
  error anywhere. Invisible on the machine that compacted; total on the other.
- **The obvious implementation corrupts real data.** "Keep the last op per
  target key" on My Notebook's real log produced a state difference against a
  full replay: node `019fdff4-…` has `node.upsert@lc149`, `node.upsert@lc152`,
  `node.delete@lc184`; keeping only the delete makes `Materializer.nodeDelete`
  `putIfAbsent` a default node, and **the deleted page reappears in the recycle
  bin** with an empty title. Same rule drops the `block.set` an `ink.strokes`
  op depends on, and `Materializer.inkStrokes` then hits `if (block == null)
  return;` — the handwriting disappears and the log looks intact.
- **The rewrite loop loses roughly one op per window boundary** if the
  straddling-line carry is dropped — measured by deliberately removing it: 77
  ops lost on the 64.56 MB log, 3 on the 1.51 MB one. A spike author
  introduced this bug in his own first draft without noticing.

[v0.13](v0.13-op-log-compaction-review.md) rejected this design twice already,
from a different direction, and every one of its five kill-shots still stands.
Two of them were independently re-reproduced this round on the *post*-Phase-1
log shape. **#54 is closed. The log stays append-only, exactly as ADR-0006
promises.**

> **The one real instance of #54's problem, and its actual fix.** Honours-2's
> 64.56 MB log is genuinely large. Its cause is 113 ink `block.set` ops holding
> 64,616 strokes in 66,204,680 bytes — **97.8% of the file**. The fix is
> pressing the existing **Shrink handwriting** button (`convertInkToBinary`),
> not a format change. It has never run on that notebook because automatic
> housekeeping requires the notebook to be *open* and Honours-2 is not
> registered in `workspace.json` at all. A button press, offline, verified,
> opt-in — see §4.3.

**When to revisit:** when a real device's post-Phase-1 log passes ~8 MB. The
number to watch is not size but **write amplification**: measured 2.55× on the
one genuinely edit-driven log (237.6 KB written for 93.1 KB of final content;
worst single block 20.1×). If it is ever taken up, the shape to reach for is
**append-only multi-member gzip** (RFC 1952 concatenated members — measured
within 6.2% of ideal at 64 KiB members, never rewrites or deletes a byte, and
therefore sidesteps every kill-shot above), *not* rotation-plus-compaction.

## 1.2 ADR-0006 §3's literal layout — **amend the ADR, do not implement it**

§3 (2026-07-27) draws `cache.onote` **inside** `MyNotebook.onotebook/`. §8
(2026-08-03, six days later) made that same directory the git repo root and the
thing Drive/OneDrive/Dropbox/Syncthing replicate. Putting a live WAL SQLite
database inside the replicated tree re-creates precisely the torn-database
hazard §2 exists to prevent.

git tolerates it — `git check-ignore -v` reports
`.gitignore:8:*.onote  cache.onote`, and `git_sync.dart:203-213` writes that
ignore file. **The four consumer providers have no per-file ignore at all.**

The code already does the right thing (the container is a sibling, or lives in
the local workspace). **The ADR is what is wrong.** Leaving the diagram as
drawn invites a future implementer to "finish the job" by moving a WAL database
into a cloud folder. §3 and spec §11's identical diagram both need amending as
part of Step 4.

## 1.3 "Mirrors go incremental" (#55) — **already done**

`mirrors.dart:67` reads, in its own doc comment: *"Copies **newer-or-missing
files only**."* Nothing to do. Close that line of #55.

## 1.4 "Backups stop snapshotting the container" (#55) — **cut, deliberately**

`mirrors.dart` decided the opposite on purpose, and its reasoning is right: *a
snapshot you have to reconstruct before you can read it is not what anyone
wants in an emergency.* It uses SQLite's own `VACUUM INTO` (`mirrors.dart:75`),
never a plain file copy of an open database. After demotion that snapshot is
**1.34 MB** (measured `cache.onote` size), so it becomes cheap rather than
something to remove. Keep it.

## 1.5 Image re-encoding — **cut from this cluster, not from the roadmap**

Two passes measured it independently and it is the biggest remaining number:
PNG images are **26.33 MB, 72% of Honours-4's content**, and re-encode to
**15.50 MB lossless WebP** (58.9%, verified by decoding all 378 back and
comparing RGBA bytes: **0 pixel mismatches**) or ~9.76 MB lossy at q88. That is
8.7× the entire yield of #54.

It is cut from *this* cluster for a hard reason: **re-encoding changes every
content hash**, and hashes appear in `page_mirror.json`, `blob_refs`,
`page_versions.snapshot` (which holds *older* page JSON naming *older* hashes),
`refs`, and every device's unsynced logs. `page_versions` is exactly the
page-scoped table with no foreign key — commit `1be2d28` exists because of that
blind spot. And **blob GC does not exist** (ADR-0007, unbuilt), so the old
blobs would simply accumulate and then be collected wrong.

Correct order, which is also what v0.4 §2.4 argues on independent grounds:
**demotion → blob GC → image re-encode.** Open it as its own task after this
cluster, with the open-format question (§7.3) answered first.

## 1.6 What replaces #54 in the backlog

**ADR-0007's blob GC.** It now has a measured target and nothing in the
codebase can free a single byte: `grep` finds no code anywhere that deletes
from the `blobs` table or from `blobs/`. Orphan blobs (rows with no `blob_refs`
row) measured: **Honours-2 18 blobs / 1,691,344 B; Honours-4 3 / 250,178 B; My
Notebook 2 / 566,810 B — 20% of that notebook's blob bytes.** Phase 1 moved the
garbage out of the log and into `blobs/`, where nothing collects it. ADR-0007's
own "Revisit triggers" section names *"a user reports Openote filling a disk"*;
the duplicates finder shipping suggests that trigger has effectively fired.

# Part 2 — What we ARE doing, and why the case changed

## 2.1 The seam the tasks were bundled across

#39/#55 bundle **a size win Phase 1 already collected** with **a correctness
win they never mention**. Split on that seam.

**The size win shrank.** v0.11 §1.2 costed the demotion when the double-store
was 63 MB of ink JSON plus 26 MB of images, and quoted 2.23× → 1.05×. Measured
today, end to end, by a spike that actually performed the migration on copies of
the real notebooks:

| Notebook | Before | After | Saved |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **Drive notebook** (shared) | 63,193,548 B | 32,578,572 B | **30,614,976 B (48.4%)** |
| **Honours-4** (local-only) | 36,689,419 B | 32,630,831 B | **4,058,588 B (11.1%)** |

The local-only case is the *majority* case — that is why `materialiseBlobs`
defers image bytes at all. So the honest framing is: **an 11% win on most
notebooks, a 48% win on the ones that sync.** Not a 2.23× → 1.05× overhaul.

**The correctness win is live, on the owner's machine, right now.** Google
Drive is replicating a WAL SQLite database:

```
G:\My Drive\Openote\
  Eric - Computing Science Honours (2).onote        31,674,368 B   ← open in openote.exe
  Eric - Computing Science Honours (2).onote-wal       247,232 B   ← replicated independently
  Eric - Computing Science Honours (2).onote-shm        32,768 B
  Eric - Computing Science Honours (2).onotebook/    31.24 MB
  Eric - Computing Science Honours.onote            35,979,264 B   ← orphan, no .onotebook
  Eric - Computing Science Honours.onote-wal         4,152,992 B   ← 4 MB of uncheckpointed pages
```

That is ADR-0006 §2's *"WAL makes it worse … can produce a torn database. This
is a live risk today, not a future one"* — happening, in production. The cause
is `Repository.moveNotebookTo` (`repository.dart:455-519`), which moves the
container **and** the log dir into the sync folder for a notebook this device
created — while the joined-notebook branch **twelve lines above it**
(`repository.dart:437`) already refuses to do exactly that, with a comment
explaining why. *Half the fix is present and correct; the other half was never
wired up.*

**And the `-wal` is not a footnote.** Measured by reading each container with
and without replaying its WAL: My Notebook's 4,128,272-byte `-wal` holds **2
whole pages, newer revisions of 6 more, 1 of 5 blobs and 5 of 22
`page_versions`**. The Drive notebook's 247 KB `-wal` holds newer revisions of
4 pages. Any migration that moves a container without checkpointing it first
throws those away, and the result passes `PRAGMA integrity_check`.

## 2.2 The op log is now a proven rebuild authority

ADR-0007 recorded the blocker as *"rebuild-from-log … has never been the
user-facing join path"*. That is now half-false — `Repository.adoptLogDirectory`
+ `AppState.syncPull` ship as the git-join path — and the remaining half was
settled empirically rather than argued. Three independent passes replayed each
notebook's log with the app's own `Materializer` and compared canonical page
JSON against the container:

| Notebook | Pages identical | Blobs present | Skipped ops | Rebuild cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Drive notebook | **329 / 329** | 491 / 491 | 0 | `readAll` 128 ms + replay 11 ms |
| Honours-2 (legacy 64.56 MB log) | **328 / 328** | 378 / 378 | 0 | `readAll` 1,638 ms + replay 14 ms |
| Honours-4 | **329 / 329** | see below | 0 | 102–120 ms |
| My Notebook | **14 / 14** | 5 / 5 | 0 | 72 ms |

Nodes: 360 in the container, 354 identical, **6 differing only in the spelling
of `kind`** (§3.3).

**But that verification is blind to the loss the migration can cause**, and
this is the single most important sentence in this document. `page_mirror` JSON
references an image only by hash, so a page whose blob *bytes* have been
destroyed is byte-identical to the same page with its bytes intact. A spike
stopped the blob backfill at 100 of 488 blobs, ran the migration to completion,
and it printed `MIGRATION COMPLETE`, left a `cache.onote` whose
`integrity_check` says `ok`, and the page-level check reported
**`same=329 differing=0`** — while **278 blob hashes had no bytes anywhere on
disk and 193 image blocks across 40 pages were permanently broken.**
`sync_shadow_test.dart` encodes exactly that page-level property and would have
gone green through it.

Every "rebuild == container" assertion in this plan therefore compares **blob
bytes**, not blob presence, and not just pages.

## 2.3 Where the log is *not* yet an authority

- **Blob coverage on local-only notebooks.** `SyncRecorder.blob`
  (`sync_recorder.dart:322`) gates bytes on
  `if (materialiseBlobs || mime == inkMimeType)`, and `materialiseBlobs` is
  `notebookIsShared(nb)` (`app_state.dart:1042`, `:1102`, `:1148`). Measured on
  Honours-4: **378 of the 488 blob hashes its log names have no bytes in
  `blobs/`** — 26.3 MB of images. A rebuild today produces a structurally
  perfect notebook (`same=329 differing=0`) in which **271 of 271 image blocks
  are broken across 44 pages**. This is Step 5.
- **This device's own log is never replayed.** `SyncRecorder.pendingForeignOps`
  opens with `if (dev == device.id) continue` (`sync_recorder.dart:412`). A
  fresh join works because a fresh device has no own log; a device that loses
  its cache and keeps its `deviceId` in `workspace.json` gets back **only what
  other devices wrote**. `rebuildContainerFromLog` still does not exist. This
  is Step 8.
- **`page_versions` has no op kind.** `database.dart` states it outright:
  *"Version history is container-local. There is no OpKind for it."* Measured
  rows: My Notebook 22, Honours-2 6, Drive 6, most notebooks 0 — 0.03–0.23 MB.
  This is Step 8's product decision (§7.1), and §3.8 proposes a mechanism that
  makes it cheap rather than a dilemma.

## 2.4 One number that is bigger than the whole cluster, and needs no format change

**324.4 MB of the owner's 409.45 MB workspace (79%) is containers whose
notebook id appears in neither `notebooks` nor `trashed` in `workspace.json`.**
Honours-2 (97.54 MB + 94.02 MB), `Honoursonepkg.onote` (92.63 MB, no
`.onotebook` at all), `Honours-3.onote` (31.67 MB), `test.onote`, and a stray
`-wal`/`-shm` pair whose `.onote` is gone.

**Correction to one investigation pass:** it reported these as invisible to the
app. They are not. `AppState.findOrphanFiles` (`app_state.dart:2626`) scans the
workspace root and every detected cloud folder, matches `.onote`, `.onotebook`
and stray `-wal`/`-shm`, and marks workspace-root ones `safeToDelete: true`
(`:2668`). `deleteOrphans` removes them. The gap is **discoverability**: it
lives behind a *"Find leftovers…"* button (`sync_dialog.dart:730`) inside the
**sync** dialog, under a section subtitled *"Sizes, paths and leftovers"*. A
student who has never set up sync will never look there.

So this is not a missing feature, it is a missing door — and it is worth more
disk than #39 and #54 combined. Open it as its own small task: surface the
existing leftovers scan from a plain **"Free up space"** entry point, in plain
words, with sizes. Do it *before* Step 7, because §3.7 has to teach the same
scan to stay silent while a migration is in progress.

# Part 3 — The work, in order

Nine steps. Each is independently shippable and independently valuable. **No
step leaves the format in a state where an interrupted upgrade loses data**,
and the three that touch bytes (5, 7, 8) each ship with their inverse, tested
in the same PR.

Steps 1–4 change no file format at all and are worth shipping even if the rest
slips forever.

```
 1  Save failures become visible          ─┐
 2  Log reader hardening                   │  no format change
 3  Vocabulary + version gates             │  ship in any order
 4  SQLite leaves the cloud folder        ─┘
 5  Blob bytes always materialised, and proved      ← +26 MB temporarily
 6  Container stops writing blobs; reads pass through
 7  Reclaim: empty the blobs table                   ← the 30.6 MB, first irreversible step
 8  Own-log rebuild, then the rename to cache.onote  ← format v2
 ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
 9  (split out entirely) Video engine on demand      ← 48.38 MB of install, touches no user file
```

## Step 1 — A save that could not be recorded says so

**What changes.** `AppState._recorderFor` and `_warmAndInstall` catch every
exception, `debugPrint`, and return `null`. `_startBlobBackfill`
(`app_state.dart:1261`) attaches `.catchError` at `:1268` that prints and
returns 0. Those are *correct today* — the container is authoritative and
shadow mode must never break saving — and they become **silent permanent loss**
the moment the log is the format. Route both into the existing `saveError`
path, with a plain-words message and the technical detail behind Advanced.

Also in this step, because it must bake before anything needs it: **give
`workspace.json` a `format` integer**, and make a build that meets a `format`
higher than its own load the registry **read-only and refuse to rewrite it**.

**What it buys.** Converts three reachable silent failures into a message. All
three are reachable *today*: an `.oplog` truncated inside a multi-byte
character makes `readAll()` throw (Step 2 — 2 of 1,487 truncation points on the
real 1.5 MB log), a permission error, a full disk, or a cloud client holding
the file. Today the container catches the fall. After Step 7 it does not.

The workspace `format` field is the guard for a hazard nobody would otherwise
see coming: `Repository._loadWorkspace` skips any entry whose file does not
exist (`if (File(file).existsSync())`), and `_saveWorkspace` then rewrites
`workspace.json` from the in-memory list. **A student who upgrades the laptop
in October and the desktop at Christmas opens the old build on a migrated
workspace, sees an empty sidebar, creates one notebook — and permanently
prunes all the others.** The guard has to be in the field *before* Step 8
moves any container, which is why it ships first.

**Migration.** None. An older build ignores an unknown JSON key.

**Proof.** Widget test: make the ops directory unwritable, and separately plant
an undecodable `*.oplog`; save a page; assert a user-visible error surfaces and
that `saveError` is non-null — not a `debugPrint`. Second test: a
`workspace.json` with `"format": 99` loads read-only and a subsequent notebook
creation does **not** rewrite the file.

**Data safety.** Crash: nothing on disk changes. Disk full: this is the step
that makes disk-full *visible*. Old build meets new file: ignores the key.
Shared-folder skew: none.

**Rollback.** Revert the commit. The `format` key is inert to older builds.

## Step 2 — Two pre-existing log-reader bugs

**What changes.** Two one-line-class fixes in `op_log.dart`:

1. `readDevice` uses `File.readAsStringSync` (`op_log.dart:181`), which decodes
   UTF-8 **strictly** and throws `FileSystemException` on a partial sequence.
   The paced `readDeviceFrom` on the identical bytes uses
   `utf8.decode(…, allowMalformed: true)` (`:269`) and is fine. `readAll`,
   `highestLamport` and `lastSeq` all route through the strict one. Make it
   match.
2. `append` writes `ops.join('\n') + '\n'` in one `writeStringSync`
   (`op_log.dart:344`). A tear one byte from the end leaves a complete JSON
   line with no terminator, and the *next* append concatenates onto it, gluing
   two records into one unparseable line. Measured: append 3 ops, drop one
   byte, append 2 more → `readDevice` returns seqs **[1, 2, 5]**. Two ops lost
   for one lost byte, where the format's own doc comment promises one. Fix:
   check the last byte before appending and prepend a newline if it is missing.

**What it buys.** Measured on the real 1,523,044-byte log: of 1,487 truncation
points at every 1 KiB boundary, **2 made `readAll()` throw**. In a minimal
317-byte reproduction using the maths symbols the owner's notes are full of
(ℝ, ∃, ∈), **6 of 316 truncation points threw**. Today that stops sync
silently. After Step 7 it is the notebook refusing to open.

**Migration.** None. **Proof.** Table-driven: truncate a generated log at every
1 KiB boundary and at every byte of a small multi-byte-dense log; assert
`readAll` never throws and returns every complete op before the cut. Separately:
append, truncate one byte, append, assert no op is lost.

**Data safety.** All four cases unchanged — this step only makes an existing
reader more tolerant. **Rollback.** Revert.

## Step 3 — Say `section_group`, and gate the envelope

**What changes.** Two vocabulary/compatibility holes, both cosmetic today and
both data-losing after Step 8.

1. **Node kind.** `SyncRecorder.node` writes `n.kind.name`, producing
   `sectionGroup`; `database.dart`'s DDL is
   `CHECK (kind IN ('section_group','section','page'))` and the published CC0
   spec §3 documents only the snake_case set. The fold maps back with
   `NodeKind.values.firstWhere((k) => k.name == n.kind, orElse: () =>
   NodeKind.page)`. Measured on **all four notebooks: 6 nodes each**, and every
   rebuild a spike ran needed exactly 6 fixups or the `INSERT` failed outright.
   Fix: write the **spec** spelling; accept both on read, forever; and make an
   *unrecognised* kind land in `Materializer.skipped` and be preserved verbatim
   rather than being coerced to `page` — a page has a `page_mirror` foreign
   key, a `level`, and completely different semantics from a section group.
2. **Envelope version and encryption.** `Op.decode` reads `j['v']` into
   `Op.version` and **never compares it to `opFormatVersion`**;
   `Materializer.apply` switches on kind alone. Measured: a hand-written
   `{"v":2,…,"op":"block.set"}` carrying the structured `{nodes:[…]}` model
   ADR-0006 §4 plans **was applied with `skipped=0`**; an `{"enc":"aes-gcm"}`
   op decoded, yielded `map={}`, and was **silently dropped with `skipped=0`**.
   `SyncRecorder.verifyPage`'s only escape hatch ("inconclusive rather than
   incomplete") keys off `skipped.isNotEmpty`, so a real divergence today is
   reported as *data loss*. Fix: `v > opFormatVersion` or `enc != 'none'` →
   `skipped`, and the notebook opens **read-only** with a plain message. The
   container already has this gate (`database.dart:130` throws on
   `user_version > 1`); the log has none.

**What it buys.** Removes the one place where Openote's open artifact uses a
vocabulary that appears nowhere in Openote's published spec — while it is still
cosmetic. It also makes Step 8's forward compatibility mechanical rather than
hopeful.

**Migration.** Logs will contain both spellings forever; the reader accepts
both forever. No rewrite of any existing log — **this plan never rewrites a
log line, anywhere.**

**Proof.** Feed a `node.upsert` with `kind: 'section_group'` and assert it
materialises as a section group; feed `kind: 'wormhole'` and assert it lands in
`skipped` and is *not* a page. Assert an op with `v: 2` and one with
`enc: 'aes-gcm'` both land in `skipped` and put the notebook read-only.

**Data safety.** Old build meets new file: an old build reading a
`section_group` kind hits `orElse → page` — which is why the reader fix must
ship at least one release before the writer flips. **Ship read-accepts-both in
release *n*, write-spec-spelling in release *n+1*.** That is the one ordering
constraint inside this step.

**Rollback.** Revert the writer half; the reader half stays forever.

## Step 4 — Get SQLite out of the cloud folder

**What changes.** `Repository.moveNotebookTo`'s created-notebook branch
(`repository.dart:455-519`) stops moving the container into the target folder.
It moves the `.onotebook` — exactly what the joined branch at `:437` already
does — and leaves the container in the local workspace. Plus:

- A one-click **"Move the notes file out of Drive"** action in the sync dialog
  for notebooks already in this state, in those words, with no mention of WAL,
  SQLite or tearing. Technical detail behind Advanced.
- Replace `moveNotebookTo`'s `File(destFile).lengthSync() != src.lengthSync()`
  check with a **SHA-256 comparison of both files**. The primitive already
  exists (blob hashing). A copy torn in the middle by a crash or a cloud client
  has the same length and passes today — and that function's own comment
  records a previous version of this check passing *while destroying the
  notebook*.
- Amend **ADR-0006 §3** and **spec §11**'s identical diagram to put
  `cache.onote` outside the synced directory (§1.2).

**What it buys.** Removes SQLite from the replicated set. Measured on the live
Drive folder: **31,954,368 B of container + `-wal` + `-shm` stops being
replicated** (63.19 MB → 31.24 MB in Drive for that notebook, −50.5%), and
ADR-0006 §2's "live risk today" goes to zero. No format change, no byte of
anyone's notes touched. **This is the step that justifies the cluster.**

**Migration.** For each notebook whose container sits in a detected cloud
folder, offer the move. Checkpoint first (`checkpointAndClose`), copy, verify
by hash, then delete the original and its sidecars. Never delete before the
hash matches.

**Proof.** A test that performs every notebook operation — create, save,
import, join, move-to-folder, mirror, git sync — and then asserts **no path
matching `*.onote*` exists anywhere under `NotebookRef.logDirPath` or under any
configured sync location**. It must fail today for the move-to-folder case;
that is the point. Second: pre-create a `-wal` with uncommitted pages, move,
assert the destination opens with identical page count *and identical page
content*.

**Data safety.** Crash mid-move: the original is deleted only after a hash
match, so a crash leaves either the original or both. Disk full: the copy
fails, the original is untouched. Old build meets new file: the container is
still a normal `.onote` at a normal path — an old build reads it fine, and the
`format` field from Step 1 covers the registry. Shared-folder skew: this step
*removes* skew; the folder now contains only append-only files.

**Rollback.** The same mover, in reverse. Nothing is deleted that cannot be
put back.

## Step 5 — Blob bytes are always materialised, and coverage is proved

**What changes.** `materialiseBlobs` becomes unconditionally true
(`sync_recorder.dart:322`; the `notebookIsShared` gate at `app_state.dart:1042`
and `:1102` goes away), and `backfillBlobs` runs on every existing notebook.
Note the trap: `backfillBlobs` **returns 0 without reading anything** when the
flag is false (`sync_recorder.dart:354`), which reads exactly like "nothing to
do" rather than "I was not allowed to look" — so **the flag must flip before
the backfill runs**, and the backfill's completion must be *asserted*, not
inferred.

Add the invariant that every later step is gated on:

```
for every hash in blob_refs:
    a file blobs/<hash> exists
    AND its bytes re-hash to <hash>
    AND SyncRecorder.missingBlobs() is empty
```

**What it buys.** Closes the 378-of-488 hole on Honours-4. Only after this is
"the log is the notebook" true for local-only notebooks as well as shared ones.
It **costs** disk in the short term: Honours-4 goes 36.69 MB → ~63 MB until
Step 7 removes the container copy.

**Migration.** Backfill runs in the background, resumable, idempotent
(`writeBlob` is temp+rename and survived every hard kill a spike threw at it,
with no `.tmp` litter). Its failures are visible from Step 1.

**Proof.** Local-only fixture with images: run the backfill with `blobs/` made
read-only, assert it reports failure (not 0) and that the container is
untouched. Then run it normally and assert the invariant above holds byte for
byte.

**Data safety.** Crash: partial `blobs/` is always a strict subset of the
container, which is still authoritative. Disk full: the invariant fails, and
nothing downstream proceeds. Old build meets new file: extra files in `blobs/`
are exactly what a shared notebook already looks like. Shared-folder skew: a
content-addressed file written twice is the same file.

**Rollback.** Delete the added files, or leave them — they are never wrong,
only redundant.

## Step 6 — The container stops writing blobs; reads pass through

**What changes.** New blob bytes go to `blobs/` only. `AppState.blob(hash)`
(`app_state.dart:206`, 16 call sites, synchronous) becomes **file-first, then
the table** as a fallback. The `blobs` table stops being written but is not
touched. The SQL surface is small and contained: 14 call sites across 5 files
(`notebook_writer` 13, `app_state` 3, `repository` 4, `database` 2,
`ink_storage` 1).

**What it buys.** Nothing on disk yet — deliberately. It puts the read path
that Step 7 depends on into the field for a full release, on real notebooks,
while the old bytes are still sitting there as a safety net. **Renaming and
de-duplicating in one release is two irreversible changes sharing one crash
window;** this step is the split.

**Migration.** None — existing rows stay.

**Proof.** Assert a brand-new notebook has **zero** `blobs` rows and that every
`blob_refs` hash resolves to a file whose SHA-256 equals its name. Assert every
image and ink block still renders on a fixture that has bytes only in the
table, only in `blobs/`, and in both.

**Data safety.** Every case is a no-op: the bytes exist in two places
throughout. **Rollback.** Revert; new blobs written during the bake are in
`blobs/`, which the fallback read path and Step 5's backfill both already
handle.

## Step 7 — Reclaim: empty the `blobs` table

**The first irreversible step.** Everything above can be reverted by reverting a
commit. This one deletes bytes.

**What changes.** `DELETE FROM blobs` + `VACUUM`, gated, crash-idempotent, with
its inverse shipped alongside.

Five things must be true, and the migration must **refuse** rather than
proceed when any is false:

1. **Step 5's invariant holds for this notebook**, byte for byte. A spike
   destroyed 193 image blocks across 40 pages by skipping this, and the
   migration printed `MIGRATION COMPLETE`.
2. **The container has been opened and checkpointed** — never renamed or
   emptied while a `-wal` holds pages. On My Notebook that `-wal` is 2 whole
   pages and newer revisions of 6 more, and the damaged result passes
   `integrity_check`.
3. **Free space ≥ 2.0× the container.** Measured: `VACUUM` on the 97,542,144 B
   container peaked at **192,787,184 B** across the `.onote` + a 95,048,432 B
   `-wal` + `-shm` (1.98×), took 1.33 s, and recovered 3 MB. A rebuild peaked at
   a 14.86 MB `-wal` for a 31 MB container. And local-only notebooks need the
   Step 5 double-store first: Honours-4's transient peak is ~63 MB from 36.69 MB
   (**1.72×**). Refuse *before* touching anything, with a plain message.
4. **`PRAGMA foreign_keys` is handled explicitly.** `database.dart:272`
   declares `hash TEXT NOT NULL REFERENCES blobs(hash)` and spec §2 requires
   foreign keys ON, so `DELETE FROM blobs` errors. **The obvious repair — add
   `ON DELETE CASCADE` — silently empties `blob_refs`, which is ADR-0007's GC
   root set, and the next housekeeping run then classifies every blob file as
   unreferenced and deletes it.** A spike had to pass
   `PRAGMA foreign_keys=OFF` to take its own measurement, which is how easy
   this is to walk into. The correct fix is to drop the constraint in the v2
   schema, deliberately, and to assert `blob_refs`' row count is **byte-
   identical** before and after.
5. **`reclaimFreeSpace` stops reporting failure as success.** Its bare `catch`
   returns 0, so an out-of-disk `VACUUM` is indistinguishable from "nothing to
   reclaim".

**What it buys.** Drive notebook 63,193,548 → 32,578,572 B (**48.4%**);
Honours-4 36,689,419 → 32,630,831 B (**11.1%**). Container 31,707,136 →
1,470,464 B by direct measurement of `DELETE FROM blobs; VACUUM`.

**Its inverse, shipped in the same PR.** `refillContainerBlobs()` — re-ingest
every hash in `blob_refs` from `blobs/` back into the table. It is only
*possible* because Step 5 proved coverage; it must be **written and tested
before Step 7 is allowed to ship**, and it is the rollback story for anyone
who hits a problem in the field.

**Data safety.** Crash: the delete is one transaction and rolls back; the
`VACUUM` either completes or leaves the original. A real `SQLITE_FULL` (code
13) was injected during a rebuild and the `BEGIN`/`COMMIT` held — **but it left
a 4,096-byte container beside a 14,864,992-byte `-wal`, and that pair passes
`notebookFileProblem` via the `appId==0`-plus-`-wal` concession**, after which a
normal open settles it into a valid, empty 73,728-byte notebook. So the
migration must write a marker file naming the step *before* it starts, and
refuse to treat a container as fresh while that marker exists. Disk full: the
§3 precheck. Old build meets new file: an emptied `blobs` table with a populated
`blobs/` is exactly what an old build sees on a joined notebook today — it
reads through `blob_refs` and finds bytes; the read-through path from Step 6 is
what makes this safe, which is why Step 6 must bake first. Shared-folder skew:
`blobs/` is content-addressed and append-only; nothing here writes to it.

**Interaction with the leftovers scan (§2.4).** `findOrphanFiles` marks any
unclaimed workspace `.onote` `safeToDelete: true`, and classes a `-wal`/`-shm`
as an orphan precisely when its `.onote` is absent — **which is the state a
migration creates for its duration**. Take a lock file, and make the scan
return nothing at all while it is held. Test that at every migration step.

**Rollback.** `refillContainerBlobs()`, plus revert.

## Step 8 — Own-log rebuild, the version-history decision, then the rename

**What changes.** Four things, in this order, in one release:

1. **`rebuildContainerFromLog(notebookId)` for real.** `pendingForeignOps`
   structurally cannot provide it — `sync_recorder.dart:412` is
   `if (dev == device.id) continue`, and a single-device notebook's entire
   history lives in exactly the file that skips. Measured cost: 102–120 ms for
   329 pages / 2,780 ops.
2. **`openOnote` must refuse to fabricate.** On a path that does not exist it
   returns a valid `Database` with 0 nodes and 0 pages, leaves a 73,728-byte
   file, and `notebookFileProblem` then reports *"looks like a notebook"*.
   `Repository._db` calls `openOnote(nb.file, …)` with **no existence check**
   (`repository.dart:366-369`), so this is reachable today whenever a
   registered container's path goes missing — an unmounted drive, a cloud
   client that evicted the file. Add `openExistingOnote`, and use it
   everywhere a notebook is *expected* to exist.
3. **`page_versions` survives the rebuild.** Recommendation, for §7.1: keep
   version history container-local — do **not** give it an op kind, because 30
   full page snapshots per page is the one thing that would genuinely make an
   append-only log grow without bound, which is the problem #54 was invented to
   solve. Instead, make the rebuild **preserve** it: copy the table out, rebuild,
   copy it back. Measured cost: 6–22 rows, 0.03–0.23 MB. That turns an
   unanswerable product dilemma into a 0.2 MB table copy. The UI still says it
   plainly: *"Earlier versions of a page are kept on this computer only."*
4. **Only then, the rename**, with the version gate below.

**The migration is a named step sequence with a marker file**, because the
scariest failure a spike found is not a crash — it is the **recovery**:

> A hard `TerminateProcess` immediately after the rename, followed by the
> obvious *"just run the migration again"*, **destroyed a 329-page notebook**.
> Step 1 of the re-run called `openOnote` on the now-missing old path, which
> fabricated an empty database; step 5 renamed it over the real `cache.onote`
> — which `File.renameSync` on Windows does **silently, with no exception**
> (verified directly: destination content `REAL NOTEBOOK CONTENT` became `new`,
> no error, source gone). Result: 73,728 bytes, 0 pages, 0 blobs,
> `integrity_check` `ok`. The same disaster reproduced from a kill one step
> later, where the crashed state was itself perfectly fine.

So: write `migrate.json` into the log directory before touching anything,
naming the step and both paths; **never rename onto an existing destination**;
and make every step resumable from the marker.

**What it buys.** The container becomes what ADR-0006 §3 always said it was —
a local, rebuildable cache — and the app can finally offer *"rebuild this
notebook from its history"* as a real repair rather than a hope.

### The version gate, stated

| Surface | Value now | After Step 8 | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Op envelope `v` | 1 | **1, unchanged** | No new op kind is added anywhere in this cluster. Spec §12: new kinds are additive and do not bump `v`. |
| Op `enc` | `none` | `none` | Gated in Step 3 so a future value stops an old build instead of being silently dropped. |
| Container `user_version` | 1 | **2** | The file's name and role change. An old build meeting a `cache.onote` must refuse, and `database.dart:130` already throws on `user_version > 1`. |
| `manifest.json` `format` | 1 | **2** | So a joining device knows which layout it is looking at. |
| `workspace.json` `format` | *absent* | **2** (introduced as 1 in Step 1) | The only mechanical guard against the old build silently pruning the registry. |

**On the format freeze.** Spec §12 promises *"From v0.2.0 onward, format v1 is
frozen."* v0.11 Part 0 lifted that on the owner's words *"No real users yet"*.
That was worth checking rather than assuming, so it was measured: the GitHub
API reports **10 release objects, 7 with published assets, and no single asset
with more than 2 downloads** (two summarisation passes over the same response
totalled 14 and 22, so treat the exact sum as low tens; the per-asset ceiling of
2 is the number that matters). Some of those are certainly the owner's own.

**Conclusion: the exposed population is at most a couple of dozen downloads,
none of whom can be contacted — so do the documented one-way v1 → v2 migration
properly (spec §12's own mechanism), and do *not* build a downgrade path.** The
in-app updater means a stranger's app will offer the newer build; the
`workspace.json` guard from Step 1 means the older build fails *safe* rather
than failing *permanent* in the meantime.

**Also required in this step, and easy to miss:** `MediaGc`'s safety argument
is written in prose that stops being true. `media_gc.dart:310-317` skips this
device's own `.oplog` and justifies it: *"The container is authoritative and the
log is written alongside it … our own log can lag our container but never lead
it."* Demotion deletes both halves of that premise. The conclusion probably
survives — a replay-to-final-state rebuild does not resurrect a removed block —
but it survives for a *different reason*, and a wrong delete here costs somebody's
lecture recording. Rewrite the comment and add the test in row C7 of the matrix.

**Data safety.** Crash: the marker file plus refuse-to-fabricate plus
never-rename-onto-a-destination. Disk full: the §3 precheck, run before the
marker is written. Old build meets new file: `user_version = 2` → refuses;
`workspace.json format = 2` → read-only registry. Shared-folder skew: the
rename happens entirely outside the synced directory (Step 4 put the container
there), so no synced file changes.

**Rollback.** A scripted, tested **`undemote`**: rename back, `user_version`
back to 1, `refillContainerBlobs()`, `workspace.json format` back to 1. Written
and tested in the same PR as the rename.

## Step 9 — Video engine on demand (split out entirely)

**Not part of this cluster.** It shares nothing with the format work but a task
number, and bundling it behind a format migration is why it has not shipped.

Measured: the video stack is **48,377,078 B of a 97,831,741 B Windows Release
bundle (49.4%)** — `libmpv-2` 29,764,622 + `libGLESv2` 7,414,088 +
`d3dcompiler_47` 4,891,080 + `vk_swiftshader` 4,808,008 + `vulkan-1` 872,776 +
`libEGL` 472,904 + the two plugin DLLs. The ANGLE half is **media_kit's, not
Flutter's**: `media_kit_libs_windows_video-1.0.11/windows/CMakeLists.txt`
downloads *both* `mpv-dev-…7z` and `ANGLE.7z` at build time into
`build/windows/x64/{libmpv,ANGLE}`. It touches **zero bytes of notebook data** —
no `media/` directory exists anywhere in the workspace or the Drive folder.

One open verification (§7.4) and one product constraint: the first-video
download must be **one click**, and must say something like *"Openote needs to
fetch a video player (about 30 MB). Get it"* — no mention of mpv, ANGLE or
codecs.

**Open it as its own task and ship it whenever.** It cannot lose anyone's notes.

# Part 4 — Things this plan deliberately leaves alone

## 4.1 Nothing rewrites a log line, ever

Not compaction, not segmentation, not a spelling fix. `Op.toJson` emits exactly
`{v,dev,seq,lc,ts,enc,op,d}`, so a rewrite through `Op.encode` drops any
envelope key a newer build wrote, and `d` is not numerically byte-stable through
`jsonDecode`/`jsonEncode` — table cells, board card geometry, code-cell output
and block transforms all still carry doubles there.

## 4.2 The `.onote` file association

`packaging/windows/*.iss` registers `Software\Classes\.onote` and
`packaging/linux/openote.xml` globs `*.onote`; `notebookFileProblem` sniffs the
SQLite magic and `application_id`, both of which a cache satisfies. Task #43
shipped the double-click-a-notebook flow. After Step 8 the container is a
local-only cache in the workspace — so what does a student double-click? That
is §7.2, and it is a genuine open question, not packaging detail. Step 8's
`user_version = 2` at least makes a double-clicked cache **refuse** rather than
silently clone itself into a second notebook.

## 4.3 The legacy notebooks on this disk

Honours-2 still holds 113 inline-ink pages, 67.05 MB of `page_mirror` and a
64.56 MB log. The ink conversion never ran on it because `_runHousekeeping`
requires the notebook to be **open**, and Honours-2 is not registered in
`workspace.json` at all. That is settled, not mysterious — but it means the
pre-Phase-1 shape can still walk into the migration if a user ever re-registers
one, which is why matrix row B3 exists and why the two migrations must compose
in either order.

The honest offer for such a notebook is a one-off, opt-in, offline, verified
**"this notebook still uses the old handwriting format — shrink it"** action.
Not a format change.

# Part 5 — The CI testing matrix

## 5.1 Four rules that make it trustworthy rather than merely long

1. **Every fixture is generated, never committed.** Model: commit `2a0a300`,
   whose Rust `onenote.rs` test module synthesises real MS-ONESTORE bytes at
   runtime. One new `app/test/support/notebook_fixture.dart` emits a real
   `.onote` + `.onotebook` pair to a requested shape — pages, ink blocks,
   images, videos, `page_versions`, op count, device count, `user_version`,
   `materialiseBlobs`, pre- or post-Phase-1 ink — seeded and deterministic,
   **each parameter named after the trap it reproduces**. Images are generated
   4×4 PNGs. **The owner's notes never enter the repo.**
2. **Every destructive cell asserts on CONTENT, not on the absence of an
   exception.** All six bugs found on 2026-08-15 passed their exception checks.
   The assertion of record is `Materializer(readAll())` == container,
   page-for-page and block-for-block, **plus blob-set equality with bytes
   compared** — because §2.2 showed the page-level property going green through
   two runs that destroyed 193 and 271 image blocks respectively.
3. **A cell that cannot fail is not a cell.** Each row ships with a recorded
   revert-check: name a mutation, record how many assertions it turns red.
4. **A skip is a failure in CI.** `initSqliteForTests` already throws under CI.
   A meta-test reads the JSON reporter and fails if the skipped count exceeds a
   number committed to the repo.

**Baseline, measured at `bb35fad`:** Dart 1,388 passed / 16 skipped / 0 failed
locally; Rust 74 passed / 0 failed. In CI it is really **~1,384 passed / 20
skipped** — five sample-gated files (45 `expect()` calls) skip because
`USERPROFILE` is unset on Linux/macOS runners, verified empirically by
re-running them with `USERPROFILE` pointed at an empty directory.

## 5.2 Fix the gate first

`.github/workflows/release.yml` runs **no tests at all** — `flutter test`,
`cargo test` and `clippy` all return zero matches — and has no `needs:` on the
CI jobs. Its own comment says *"cut tags only from a green master: this
workflow does not re-run tests (CI gates every push)"*. Because `ci.yml`'s
`push:` is unfiltered it does fire on the tag, but **nothing blocks the draft
release if it goes red. The gate is a human convention, not a mechanism.**
One line, in the same PR as Step 1.

# Part 6 — Rollback, per step

| Step | Reversible? | How |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Save failures visible | yes | revert; `workspace.json`'s `format` key is inert to older builds |
| 2 Log reader hardening | yes | revert; no on-disk change |
| 3 Vocabulary + gates | yes (writer half) | revert the writer; **the reader accepts both spellings forever**, so a rolled-back build still reads new logs |
| 4 SQLite leaves the cloud | yes | the same mover, in reverse; nothing is deleted before a hash match |
| 5 Blobs always materialised | yes | delete the added files, or leave them — content-addressed files are never wrong, only redundant |

# Part 7 — Open questions for the owner

Only four remain. Everything else in the investigation was settled by
measuring, including the format-freeze question (§ Step 8: ≤2 downloads per
asset).

## 7.1 Is *"Earlier versions of a page are kept on this computer only"* acceptable?

`page_versions` is the one user-visible feature with no representation in the
log. §3 Step 8 recommends **keeping it container-local and preserving it across
a rebuild by copying the table out and back** — measured at 6–22 rows and
0.03–0.23 MB, so the mechanism is nearly free. What needs your call is the
*promise*: is it acceptable that page history does not follow a notebook to a
second machine and does not survive a full disk loss? The alternative — a
`page.version` op kind — puts 30 full page snapshots per page into an
append-only log, which is the one thing that would make it grow without bound.

## 7.2 What does a student double-click after the demotion?

Task #43 shipped the double-click flow and the installers register `.onote`.
After Step 8 the thing that syncs is a **directory**, and Windows has no
equivalent association. Options: register a directory verb; ship a small pointer
file; or drop double-click for shared notebooks and lean on the join flow. The
product rule (*"a year 10 student wont know…"*) makes this a first-class
question, not packaging detail. Step 8's `user_version = 2` makes a
double-clicked cache *refuse* — which is safe but is not an answer.

## 7.3 Does the demotion keep a third-party-openable single file at all?

Spec §10 sells the `.onote` container as the open format. Spec §11 already says
the openness guarantee **moves to the log** — but §10 has not been rewritten to
match, and "anyone can read and write `.onote` files" is the licensing
invariant CI enforces via `cargo deny`. Is the ordered union of `ops/*.oplog` +
`blobs/<sha256>` the artifact we tell third parties to read, and do we owe them
a one-file export instead? This also decides §1.5's WebP question later (spec §1
goal 1 requires "a well-known open format"; WebP is RFC 9649 and universally
decodable, but it changes what a minimal third-party reader must support).

## 7.4 Two things that gate work rather than shape it

- **Is one human macOS pass available before Step 8 ships?** macOS has never
  been run by a human (TESTING.md §5), and ADR-0006 §6 says macOS may present
  `.onotebook` as a bundle while the layout MUST NOT change. Presentation
  versus layout is exactly the kind of structural question this project has
  been punished for assuming. **Recommendation: gate Step 8 on it.**
- **Does removing media_kit actually reclaim all 48.38 MB, or does Flutter's
  Windows embedder need `libEGL`/`libGLESv2` independently?** The five ANGLE
  DLLs are all dated 2022 and sit under a directory media_kit created, which is
  strong evidence — but that is inference. The only honest answer is a release
  build with the dependency removed and the app launched. One afternoon, and it
  is Step 9's whole business case.

# Part 8 — Summary

**Cut:** #54 entirely (segments, gzip, snapshots, compaction — 0.00–0.16%
superseded bytes, 1.30 MB of gzip on a 30 MB synced set, a 54 ms replay the
snapshot was meant to fix, and reproduced silent multi-block loss on the
owner's real log); ADR-0006 §3's literal cache-inside-the-synced-directory
layout (amend the ADR instead); "mirrors go incremental" (already done);
"backups stop snapshotting the container" (a deliberate decision, and demotion
makes that snapshot 1.34 MB); image re-encoding (real — 10.83 MB verified
lossless — but blocked behind blob GC).

**Kept and re-argued:** the demotion, as a correctness fix. Its byte case is
**48.4% on a shared notebook and 11.1% on a local-only one**, not the
2.23× → 1.05× it was sold as. Its real payoff is removing a live WAL SQLite
database from Google Drive.

**Added to the backlog in #54's slot:** ADR-0007's blob GC (18 orphans /
1.69 MB in one container, 20% of another notebook's blob bytes, and nothing in
the codebase can free one), and a plain **"Free up space"** door onto the
leftovers scan that already exists (324 MB of a 409 MB workspace, worth more
than this entire cluster).

**Split out:** the video engine (48.38 MB of a 97.83 MB bundle, zero user
files).

The one sentence to keep from all of it: **every way a spike destroyed a
notebook was in the bookkeeping around the migration — the recovery path, the
backfill's swallowed error, the un-checkpointed WAL — and never in the format
change itself.** That is where the work is.
